## Neural Networks on Tabular Data

Deep learning has taken over image and NLP tasks, but **tabular data is different**. Credit risk datasets have:

- Moderate sample sizes (10k–10M rows)
- Mixed types (continuous, ordinal, categorical)
- Strong domain-logic constraints
- Regulatory interpretability requirements

Research (e.g., "Why do tree-based models still outperform deep learning on tabular data?", Grinsztajn 2022) shows that gradient boosting often matches or beats NNs on tabular data. However, NNs offer unique capabilities:

- **Entity embeddings** for high-cardinality categoricals (postcode, employer, industry)
- **Multi-task learning** (predict PD and LGD jointly)
- **Temporal models** (LSTM for payment sequence data)

## Entity Embeddings

Instead of one-hot encoding a categorical variable with 10,000 levels, entity embeddings map each category to a learned dense vector of size $d \ll N$:

$$
e_{\text{postcode}} = E_{\text{postcode}}[\text{index}] \in \mathbb{R}^d
$$

The embedding dimension is typically $d = \min(50, \lceil N/2 \rceil)$.

## Platt Scaling: Calibrating Neural Network Outputs

Neural networks often produce poorly calibrated probabilities — the raw softmax output may not correspond to empirical default rates. **Platt scaling** fits a logistic regression on the model outputs to recalibrate:

$$
\hat{p}_{\text{calib}} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(A \cdot f(x) + B)}}
$$

Where $A$ and $B$ are fitted on a held-out calibration set.
