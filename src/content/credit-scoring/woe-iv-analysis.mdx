## Weight of Evidence

Weight of Evidence (WoE) is a monotonic encoding of categorical or binned continuous features. For each bin $b$:

$$
WoE_b = \ln\left(\frac{\text{Distribution of Events}}{\text{Distribution of Non-Events}}\right) = \ln\left(\frac{p_b^{\text{bad}}}{p_b^{\text{good}}}\right)
$$

Where:
- $p_b^{\text{bad}}$ = fraction of all defaults in bin $b$
- $p_b^{\text{good}}$ = fraction of all non-defaults in bin $b$

**Interpretation:**
- WoE > 0: bin has higher proportion of defaults than average → **risky** bin
- WoE < 0: bin has lower proportion of defaults than average → **safe** bin
- WoE = 0: bin has same default rate as portfolio average

## Information Value

IV summarizes the **total predictive power** of a feature across all its bins:

$$
IV = \sum_{b} (p_b^{\text{bad}} - p_b^{\text{good}}) \times WoE_b
$$

### IV Interpretation Rules

| IV Range | Predictive Power |
|---|---|
| < 0.02 | Useless — exclude from model |
| 0.02 – 0.10 | Weak predictor |
| 0.10 – 0.30 | Medium predictor |
| 0.30 – 0.50 | Strong predictor |
| > 0.50 | Very strong — check for data leakage |

<Callout type="warning" title="IV > 0.5 Warning">
An IV exceeding 0.5 almost always indicates data leakage — the feature is unknowingly capturing the target variable. Classic example: including a post-default flag in a pre-default model.
</Callout>
