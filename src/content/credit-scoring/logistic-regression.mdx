## Why Logistic Regression?

For decades, logistic regression has been the dominant approach to retail credit scoring. It offers:

- **Interpretable coefficients**: Each variable's contribution is a log-odds ratio
- **Regulatory acceptance**: SR 11-7 / SS1/23 and EBA guidelines explicitly support it
- **Calibrated probabilities**: The model output is a valid PD estimate
- **Stability**: Less prone to overfitting than complex ML models on small samples

Despite the rise of gradient boosting models, logistic regression remains the backbone of most production scorecards at regulated institutions.

## The Logistic Model

We model the log-odds of default as a linear function of features:

$$
\log\left(\frac{PD}{1 - PD}\right) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_1 + \beta_2 x_2 + \cdots + \beta_p x_p
$$

Solving for PD:

$$
PD = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(\beta_0 + \boldsymbol{\beta}^\top \mathbf{x})}}
$$

### Interpreting Coefficients

A coefficient $\beta_j$ represents the change in **log-odds** for a one-unit increase in $x_j$, holding all else constant. The **odds ratio** is $e^{\beta_j}$:

- $\beta_j > 0$: feature increases default probability (riskier)
- $\beta_j < 0$: feature decreases default probability (safer)
- $e^{\beta_j}$: multiplicative factor on the odds of default
