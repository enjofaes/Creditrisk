## From Model to Scorecard

A logistic regression produces a predicted probability — but practitioners and relationship managers often prefer to communicate risk as a **score** between 0 and 1000. The PDO (Points to Double the Odds) method transforms log-odds into an integer score.

### Scaling Equations

Given a target odds at a reference score and a PDO parameter:

$$
\text{Factor} = \frac{PDO}{\ln(2)}, \quad \text{Offset} = \text{Score}_0 - \text{Factor} \cdot \ln(\text{Odds}_0)
$$

The final score:

$$
\text{Score} = \text{Offset} + \text{Factor} \cdot \ln\left(\frac{1 - PD}{PD}\right)
$$

Convention: **higher score = lower risk** (better borrowers).

### Per-Feature Points

For each feature $j$ with WoE-encoded value $x_j^{WoE}$:

$$
\text{Points}_j = -(\beta_j \cdot x_j^{WoE} + \beta_0 / p) \times \text{Factor}
$$

Where $p$ is the number of features. This decomposes the total score into individual feature contributions that sum exactly to the total score.
