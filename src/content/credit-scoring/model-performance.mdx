## The Discrimination–Calibration Distinction

Model performance has two separate dimensions:

1. **Discrimination**: Does the model correctly rank borrowers from safest to riskiest?
2. **Calibration**: Are the predicted probabilities numerically accurate?

A model can discriminate well but be poorly calibrated (e.g., all PDs are 3× too high). Both dimensions must be monitored.

## Gini Coefficient and AUC

The **Gini coefficient** is the most widely used discrimination metric in credit risk:

$$
\text{Gini} = 2 \times AUC - 1
$$

It ranges from 0 (no discrimination) to 1 (perfect discrimination). Industry benchmarks:

| Portfolio | Acceptable Gini |
|---|---|
| Retail consumer | > 0.40 |
| Small business | > 0.30 |
| Large corporate | > 0.20 |

## Population Stability Index (PSI)

PSI measures whether the **score distribution** has shifted between development (training) and monitoring (deployment) periods. A shifted distribution may render model performance invalid.

$$
PSI = \sum_b \left(A_b - E_b\right) \cdot \ln\left(\frac{A_b}{E_b}\right)
$$

Where $A_b$ = actual (current) distribution, $E_b$ = expected (development) distribution.

| PSI | Action |
|---|---|
| < 0.10 | No action required |
| 0.10 – 0.25 | Investigate and monitor |
| > 0.25 | Model likely needs recalibration or rebuild |
