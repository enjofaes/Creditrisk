## F-IRB vs A-IRB: Key Differences

| Dimension | F-IRB (Foundation) | A-IRB (Advanced) |
|---|---|---|
| PD | Bank estimates | Bank estimates |
| LGD | Supervisor prescribes | Bank estimates |
| EAD | Supervisor prescribes | Bank estimates |
| Maturity | Fixed at 2.5 yrs | Bank estimates |
| Data history | 5 years minimum (PD) | 7 years (PD), 7 years (LGD/EAD) |
| Regulatory burden | Lower | Higher (model review) |
| Capital benefit | Moderate | Potentially higher |

<Callout type="warning" title="A-IRB Data Requirements">
The 7-year LGD/EAD history must cover a complete credit cycle including a downturn period. Supervisors require evidence that internal estimates adequately reflect stressed economic conditions — not just benign periods.
</Callout>

## Minimum Requirements for IRB Approval

Supervisors assess IRB applications against a checklist (e.g., ECB Guide to Internal Models, TRIM criteria):

### 1. Rating System Design
- Must differentiate risk meaningfully (at least 7–8 grade bins for performing, 1 default grade)
- Grades must be assigned consistently across business lines
- Overrides must be tracked and limited (< 10-15% of portfolio)

### 2. Data Requirements
- **PD**: Minimum 5 years of default experience, 7 years preferred
- **LGD**: Minimum 7 years, must include downturn observations
- **PD estimation**: At least 1 full credit cycle, TTC adjustment documented

### 3. Use Test
The rating system must be used in **material credit decisions** — pricing, limit setting, reserving. A model built only for regulatory capital fails the use test.

### 4. Margin of Conservatism (MoC)

MoC is an add-on to PD (and LGD) estimates to account for uncertainty in historical data:

$$
PD_{\text{regulatory}} = PD_{\text{estimated}} + MoC
$$

MoC increases with:
- Fewer years of data
- Data quality issues
- Model deficiencies not fully corrected

## Specialised Lending: Supervisory Slotting

For project finance, object finance, and commodities (collectively "specialised lending"), A-IRB is often not achievable due to insufficient default data. Basel prescribes **supervisory slotting** — ratings are mapped to five categories:

| Category | Risk Weight |
|---|---|
| Strong | 70% |
| Good | 90% |
| Satisfactory | 115% |
| Weak | 250% |
| Default | 0% (separate treatment) |

<Callout type="tip" title="Practical Implication">
Banks financing infrastructure, real estate development, or shipping often cannot build A-IRB models due to small sample sizes and long recovery periods. Supervisory slotting provides a pragmatic regulatory framework, but the risk weights are deliberately conservative.
</Callout>
