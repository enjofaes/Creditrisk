## The Three Pillars

Basel II/III structures bank regulation around three mutually reinforcing pillars:

| Pillar | Purpose | Key Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **Pillar 1** | Minimum capital requirements | Credit, market, operational risk |
| **Pillar 2** | Supervisory review | ICAAP, stress testing, model governance |
| **Pillar 3** | Market discipline | Public disclosure of risk exposures |

This chapter focuses on **Pillar 1 credit risk capital** — the amount of regulatory capital a bank must hold against its credit exposures.

## Capital Approaches

Basel offers a hierarchy of approaches:

1. **Standardised Approach (SA)**: External credit ratings (or Basel prescribed risk weights) determine capital charges. Simple, but coarse.
2. **Foundation IRB (F-IRB)**: Banks estimate PD; supervisors prescribe LGD and EAD.
3. **Advanced IRB (A-IRB)**: Banks estimate PD, LGD, and EAD, subject to minimum data requirements.

<Callout type="info" title="Basel III Finalisation (CRR3/Basel IV)">
Basel III finalisation (fully effective January 2025 in the EU via CRR3) introduced an output floor: IRB capital charges cannot fall below 72.5% of the standardised approach. This limits the capital benefit of sophisticated IRB models relative to the simple SA.
</Callout>

## The IRB Risk-Weight Formula

For corporate/retail exposures under IRB, the risk weight $RW$ is:

$$
K = LGD \cdot N\left(\frac{N^{-1}(PD)}{\sqrt{1-R}} + \sqrt{\frac{R}{1-R}} \cdot N^{-1}(0.999)\right) - LGD \cdot PD
$$

$$
RW = K \times MA \times 12.5
$$

Where:
- $R$ is the asset correlation (Basel-prescribed for different asset classes)
- $MA$ is the maturity adjustment
- $N^{-1}(\cdot)$ is the inverse normal CDF
- The 0.999 quantile corresponds to a **99.9% confidence level** (1-in-1000 year stress)
