## The IFRS 9 Framework

IFRS 9 replaced IAS 39 in January 2018, fundamentally changing how banks provision for credit losses. The key shift: from **incurred loss** (provision only when default has occurred) to **expected credit loss** (provision based on forward-looking probability of future default).

### The Three Stages

| Stage | Trigger | Provision Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1** | No significant credit deterioration | 12-month ECL |
| **Stage 2** | Significant Increase in Credit Risk (SICR) | Lifetime ECL |
| **Stage 3** | Credit-impaired (defaulted) | Lifetime ECL |

The transition from Stage 1 to Stage 2 is the most analytically challenging — and the most impactful for provision volatility.

## Significant Increase in Credit Risk (SICR)

SICR is met when, since origination, the credit risk on a financial instrument has **increased significantly**. The standard doesn't prescribe specific thresholds — banks must exercise judgement.

### Common SICR Indicators

**Quantitative (primary)**:
- PD has increased by more than a threshold multiple (e.g., 2× or 3×) since origination
- Lifetime PD exceeds an absolute threshold

**Qualitative (secondary)**:
- Borrower placed on watchlist
- Covenant breaches
- External credit rating downgrade
- Forbearance granted
- Industry deterioration

<Callout type="info" title="The 30-Day Rebuttable Presumption">
IFRS 9 includes a rebuttable presumption that SICR has occurred when payments are more than 30 days past due. Banks can rebut this (e.g., administrative delays) but must have strong evidence.
</Callout>

## Expected Credit Loss Calculation

$$
ECL_{12m} = PD_{12m} \times LGD \times EAD \times D
$$

$$
ECL_{\text{lifetime}} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} PD_{t|t-1} \times LGD_t \times EAD_t \times D_t
$$

Where $D_t$ is the discount factor and $PD_{t|t-1}$ is the marginal (conditional) PD at time $t$.
