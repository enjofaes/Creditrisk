## The Validation Framework

Model validation is a three-part process required by regulators in every major jurisdiction:

1. **Conceptual soundness** — Is the methodology theoretically appropriate?
2. **Ongoing monitoring** — Does the model perform as expected in production?
3. **Outcomes analysis** — Do model predictions match actual outcomes?

The US Federal Reserve's **SR 11-7** (Guidance on Model Risk Management) and the UK PRA's **SS1/23** (Model Risk Management) are the two most influential frameworks.

## Conceptual Soundness Review

Conceptual soundness assesses the model's theoretical foundation, data, and implementation:

| Area | What Validators Check |
|---|---|
| Methodology | Is the statistical approach appropriate for the risk type? |
| Data | Is training data representative? Sufficient defaults? |
| Implementation | Does code match documented methodology? |
| Assumptions | Are key assumptions documented and stress-tested? |
| Limitations | Are limitations documented and mitigated? |

<Callout type="important" title="Common Conceptual Soundness Findings">
1. Target variable definition inconsistent with regulatory definition of default
2. Development data from a benign economic period — no stress coverage
3. Survivorship bias: only accounts that were originated are in the development sample
4. Data leakage: post-default information inadvertently included in features
</Callout>
