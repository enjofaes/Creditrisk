## Two Philosophies: TTC vs PIT

One of the most important decisions in PD estimation is the **time horizon** and **economic conditioning** of the estimate. Two frameworks dominate:

### Through-the-Cycle (TTC)

TTC PDs are designed to be **stable across the economic cycle**. They represent the average default rate over a full credit cycle, abstracting away short-term fluctuations in economic conditions.

- Used by Basel IRB models for **capital adequacy** — regulators want capital to be stable, not pro-cyclically released in booms and rebuilt in busts
- Long estimation windows (7–10+ years including at least one downturn)
- Slower to update when macroeconomic conditions change

### Point-in-Time (PIT)

PIT PDs reflect the **current state of the economy** and the borrower's current financial condition.

- Used for **IFRS 9 provisioning**, pricing, and limit management
- More sensitive to economic downturns — provisions surge during recessions
- Requires macroeconomic conditioning (linkage to GDP, unemployment, etc.)

<Callout type="warning" title="Regulatory Distinction">
Using a TTC PD in your IFRS 9 model is a common modelling error. IFRS 9 requires forward-looking, PIT estimates. Using TTC PDs will systematically under-provision in downturns and over-provision in benign conditions.
</Callout>

## The Merton / KMV Structural Model

The structural approach to PD treats equity as a **call option on the firm's assets**. The firm defaults when its asset value falls below its debt obligations at maturity.

### Key Relationships

Let $V_A$ = asset value, $\sigma_A$ = asset volatility, $D$ = face value of debt, $T$ = time horizon, $r$ = risk-free rate.

The equity value is:

$$
V_E = V_A \cdot N(d_1) - D e^{-rT} \cdot N(d_2)
$$

Where:

$$
d_1 = \frac{\ln(V_A/D) + (r + \sigma_A^2/2)T}{\sigma_A \sqrt{T}}, \quad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma_A\sqrt{T}
$$

The **risk-neutral PD** is $N(-d_2)$.

The **Distance to Default (DD)** — Moody's KMV's key metric — measures how many standard deviations the asset value is from the default point:

$$
DD = \frac{V_A - D}{V_A \cdot \sigma_A}
$$
