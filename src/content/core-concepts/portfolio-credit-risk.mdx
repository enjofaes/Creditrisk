## Why Single-Loan EL Is Not Enough

Knowing the expected loss on each loan is necessary but insufficient for portfolio risk management. Loans in the same sector, geography, or economic class can default together — this **correlation** transforms individual risks into systemic portfolio risk.

A portfolio of 1,000 uncorrelated loans, each with PD = 1%, has a very stable aggregate loss distribution. But if those loans are all to oil & gas companies and oil prices collapse, they may default simultaneously. This correlation is the essence of **credit concentration risk**.

## Asset Correlation

The Basel IRB framework uses an asset correlation parameter $\rho$ that captures the degree to which firms' fortunes move together due to shared systematic risk factors.

$$
R^2 = \rho = 0.12 \cdot \frac{1 - e^{-50 \cdot PD}}{1 - e^{-50}} + 0.24 \cdot \left(1 - \frac{1 - e^{-50 \cdot PD}}{1 - e^{-50}}\right)
$$

This Basel formula shows that **low-PD borrowers have higher correlation** (they are mostly affected by systematic risk), while **high-PD borrowers are more affected by idiosyncratic factors**.

## The Gaussian Copula Framework

The Gaussian copula became the industry standard (and notoriously contributed to the 2008 crisis). The idea: defaults are driven by latent normal variables that are correlated across firms.

Firm $i$ defaults if its asset return $R_i$ falls below a threshold $\Phi^{-1}(PD_i)$:

$$
R_i = \sqrt{\rho} \cdot Z + \sqrt{1-\rho} \cdot \epsilon_i
$$

Where $Z$ is the common market factor and $\epsilon_i$ is idiosyncratic noise.

## Concentration Risk: Herfindahl-Hirschman Index

The **HHI** measures portfolio concentration. For a portfolio of $n$ exposures with weights $w_i$:

$$
HHI = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i^2
$$

| HHI Range | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| < 0.01 | Highly diversified |
| 0.01 – 0.10 | Moderate concentration |
| 0.10 – 0.25 | Significant concentration |
| > 0.25 | Highly concentrated |
