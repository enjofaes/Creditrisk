## Loss Given Default (LGD)

LGD measures the economic loss as a fraction of EAD, conditional on default having occurred:

$$
LGD = 1 - \frac{\text{Recovered Amount (NPV)}}{EAD}
$$

The recovered amount must be discounted back to the default date — recovery processes take time (months to years for corporate workouts), and the time value of money matters.

### Workout LGD vs Market LGD

**Workout LGD** (also called *ultimate LGD*): Follow the loan through the entire recovery process — collateral liquidation, legal costs, administrative expenses — and calculate the actual loss once the workout concludes. This is the Basel regulatory standard.

**Market LGD**: Use the market price of defaulted debt (distressed debt trading prices) shortly after default as a proxy for recovery value. Faster to compute, but sensitive to market liquidity conditions.

<Callout type="tip" title="LGD Modelling Approaches">
For regulatory capital, supervisors generally prefer workout LGD because it reflects actual economic outcomes. For IFRS 9, practitioners often blend market and workout approaches, particularly for traded corporate debt.
</Callout>

### Key Drivers of LGD

| Factor | Effect on LGD |
|---|---|
| Collateral quality | Higher quality → lower LGD |
| Seniority in capital structure | Senior secured → lower LGD |
| Macroeconomic cycle | Downturn → higher LGD (correlated with PD) |
| Industry sector | Real estate → lower; unsecured consumer → higher |
| Loan-to-value ratio | Higher LTV → higher LGD for secured loans |

### LGD Distributions

LGD is notoriously bimodal: many defaults result in near-full recovery (LGD ≈ 0) while others result in near-complete loss (LGD ≈ 1). This makes standard regression approaches poorly calibrated — beta regression or mixture models are preferable.

## Exposure at Default (EAD)

EAD is the outstanding balance at the moment of default. For **drawn term loans**, this is simply the outstanding principal. For **revolving credit facilities**, borrowers often draw down available limits as financial distress approaches — a phenomenon called "panic drawdowns."

### Credit Conversion Factor (CCF)

For undrawn commitments, EAD is estimated using the CCF:

$$
EAD = \text{Drawn Balance} + CCF \times \text{Undrawn Commitment}
$$

A CCF of 75% means that, on average, borrowers draw down 75% of their unused commitment before defaulting. Basel F-IRB prescribes regulatory CCFs; A-IRB banks estimate their own.
